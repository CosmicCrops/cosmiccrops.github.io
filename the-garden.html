The story

Atlanta runs on systems most people never think about — until the week they all stop. When the grid fails, a city of millions discovers how much of civilization was held together by one fragile thing: a functioning water system, and the people who keep it running.

Marcus is one of those people — a water-plant operator and lifelong prepper who saw the cracks years before anyone else believed him. Now the future he prepared for is here, and it is meaner than he imagined. His son Kameo is across a collapsing city. Getting to him means crossing a landscape of survivalists, opportunists, and ordinary people whom disaster has pushed together — and deciding, again and again, what a man owes strangers when there is nothing left to spare.

Told in two voices across the same collapsing summer, The Garden is a story about devotion under pressure: what grows when everything else is stripped away, and what it costs to protect it.

An adult novel — violence, loss, and hard choices. Book Two is in the works.

The Godzilla

Every apocalypse has a monster. In The Garden, it isn’t a creature — it’s a Godzilla made of greed and entropy, stomping through every system people trusted to hold.

The survivors can’t shoot it. They can only decide what they’ll feed it — and what they’ll refuse to.
